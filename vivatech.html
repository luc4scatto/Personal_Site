Vivatech
Interactive Experience

Role
Built the real-time experience
Timeline
2026, for Vivatech Paris
Status
Shown live at the event

Four days live at an event of over 200,000 visitors. What held people at the screen was the gestures: the whole experience answered the hand, with nothing to touch.

At Vivatech 2026 - the tenth edition of Europe's biggest tech event - LVMH brought ten of its Maisons to the Dream Gallery pavilion in Paris, showing how technology amplifies craftsmanship across the whole value chain.

For the Thélios space I developed the real-time interactive experience: a live 3D showcase of eyewear digital twins, built with TouchDesigner and Unreal Engine, letting visitors explore frames, materials and details up close as they interact with the installation.

How it works

Nothing in the booth is touched. A webcam follows one hand and the visitor's head, TouchDesigner turns that into gestures, and Unreal Engine answers in real time - both running on the same machine.

  1. Webcam 1080p, one hand and the head
  2. TouchDesigner MediaPipe hand and pose tracking, gestures built from live data channels (CHOPs)
  3. OSC A lightweight message protocol: one message per gesture channel, over localhost
  4. Unreal Engine 5.7 Levels, sequences and UI driven by those messages

The visitor's path

  1. Thumbs up An attract loop plays until someone holds a thumb up to start.
  2. One or two fingers Held up to pick one of the two Maisons.
  3. Pick, zoom, grab At a workbench the frame lies in parts: one finger picks, two zoom in, a closed fist puts it back together, each gesture playing its own assembly sequence.
  4. Pinch, move, flip A gesture opens free exploration: pinch and hold to take the frame, move to turn it, push forward to zoom, flip the hand to change color.
  5. Thumbs up again When the session runs long, a thumbs up keeps it going. Otherwise it returns to the loop.

The interesting part

Hold to confirm

Every choice fills a round progress bar while the gesture is held, so a hand passing through a crowded booth can't trigger anything by accident.

Tuned on site

Every range and threshold lives in one control panel in TouchDesigner, so tracking could be recalibrated to the booth's distance and light without touching the network.

Training built in

Nobody reads instructions at a fair. Free exploration opens with four short video steps, and each one only moves on once the visitor has done the gesture.
